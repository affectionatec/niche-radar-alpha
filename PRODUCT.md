# Product

## Register

product

## Users

- Solo founders and indie hackers looking for validated product opportunities.
- A single self-hosting operator who configures sources and LLM providers.
- Builders reviewing pipeline evidence, costs, verdicts, and generated reports.

## Product Purpose

Niche Radar watches public signals across many sources, extracts concrete pain,
clusters it into candidate niches, and produces ranked opportunities with
evidence and build-ready briefs. The product should make an automated pipeline
inspectable without pretending uncertain research is certain.

## Brand Personality

Technical, investigative, and exact. The dark monochrome interface resembles a
purpose-built research instrument: sharp edges, measured spacing, monospace
commands, and no decorative color. It is calm enough for long reviews and direct
enough for operational work.

## Anti-references

- Colorful startup dashboards with pill badges and gradient calls to action.
- Consumer trend apps that hide evidence behind optimistic scores.
- Dense enterprise navigation with nested sidebars and icon noise.
- Cinematic AI interfaces whose motion distracts from pipeline state.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence before verdict.** The interface should reveal sources, pipeline
   state, and uncertainty before asking the operator to act.
2. **Monochrome is a constraint, not a shortcut.** Hierarchy comes from type,
   spacing, opacity, and sharp borders.
3. **Commands feel deliberate.** Primary actions use the existing uppercase
   monospace language and clear irreversible-state copy.
4. **Automation stays inspectable.** Loading, failure, partial completion, and
   cost must be visible in plain language.
5. **Single-operator focus.** Login is an entry checkpoint, not a multi-tenant
   account system or onboarding funnel.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG AA contrast and keyboard-visible focus.
- Status always includes text, never color alone.
- Minimum touch targets are 44px.
- Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
